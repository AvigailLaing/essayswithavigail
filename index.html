How It Works

How I actually help students.

Parents usually ask what happens on the calls, so here it is. If you want to write an essay that goes in the yes pile, you have to write with strategy, not just a story. I call this the Yes Pile System. It moves through five stages, and depending on the package we cover them across four sessions or eight, at whatever pace your student needs. It is the same process that got me into every school I applied to. Your student stays the author the whole way through. I will help shape a sentence when one is stuck, but the essay is theirs, in their voice, built out of their own memories.

  1. 01
    Inventory
    What do I actually have to write about?

    We go through all seven Common App prompts out loud, no filtering and no judging, and I take notes live in a shared doc so your student never has to remember what they said. You cannot pick a topic from a list of topics. You pick it from a list of moments.

  2. 02
    Angle
    Which idea can carry 650 words, and what is it about?

    A story is not a thesis. This is where we find the one idea underneath your moment, the thing the essay is actually arguing about who you are.

  3. 03
    Architecture
    What goes in each paragraph?

    Before you write a word, you know what every paragraph has to do and what it promises the next one. This is the step that stops the endless rewriting.

  4. 04
    Evidence
    Am I showing this, or just claiming it?

    We go line by line and replace anything you have asserted with something you can prove. This is also the pass that makes an essay read like a person instead of a machine.

  5. 05
    Screen
    Is this actually finished?

    We read it one final time against the same questions an admissions officer is asking. Your student submits knowing it is done, rather than hoping.

Your student finishes with an essay that is done, and genuinely theirs.

And they keep the method, so the next essay, the supplements and the scholarship applications all get easier.

Getting started
When should we start?

Most students start the summer before senior year, and things get tight around the November 1 deadlines. Earlier is calmer, and it means we can sit with two or three ideas instead of committing to the first one. That said, I have taken students from a blank page to submitted in under two weeks, so if you are reading this in October it is not too late.

Which option should we pick?

If your student does not know what to write about, book calls. Finding the story is the part that is genuinely hard alone, and it is the part I am best at.

If they know the topic and just need it to be good, take the one call then written edits. If they already have a draft, written edits on their own are enough. There is a chooser above the packages that sorts this in one click.

How do I book?

Everything books through my store, and you pick your own time on the calendar there. No consultation call, no form, no waiting for me to email you back a quote. If you are buying a package you choose the first session and we schedule the rest together on that call.

We are not in the US. Does that work?

Yes. I coach students across a lot of time zones and every session is on video, so we just find a slot that works. If the time difference is genuinely brutal, the written edit options are built for exactly that, since there is nothing to schedule at all.

How the sessions work
What actually happens on a call?

If your student does not have a topic yet, I ask about their life until they say something that surprises them, and by the end of the call they have a real idea and a full outline to write from. I take notes live in a shared document so they never have to remember what we said.

If they already have a draft, they send it over beforehand and we go through it together line by line, so they leave with it actually revised rather than just critiqued.

What is in a written review?

Every review is three passes over the same draft. First, is it answering the question and is the story actually the right one. Second, is it showing rather than claiming, which is where most of the work happens, and where I mark every sentence that tells me something instead of proving it. Third, the line pass: clarity, rhythm, opening and closing, and a check that it does not read as AI.

You get it back within 48 hours with comments in the document, not a summary email.

Can a parent sit in on the sessions?

You are welcome to, and some parents do for the first one. I will say honestly that most students open up more when it is just us, and the whole method depends on them telling me things they have not told anyone. If you would rather not sit in, I will send you the notes after every call so you can see exactly what we covered.

Do you talk to me or to my student?

The sessions are with your student, because they are the one writing. Scheduling, payment and anything you want to ask me comes to you. If you would like the notes after each call, say so at the start and I will copy you every time.

Money and scheduling
Do the sessions expire?

No. Never. If you buy a four session package in July and your student goes quiet until October, those sessions are still there. Applications do not run on a tidy schedule and I am not going to punish you for that.

What if we need to move a call?

Give me 24 hours notice and we move it, no problem. You can reschedule from the confirmation email or just message me.

What if we miss one?

A missed call without notice is not rescheduled, it moves you on to the next call in your package. I hold that slot open in an evening that was fully booked, so it is the one thing I have to be firm about. Nothing is lost though, since the remaining sessions never expire.

Is the email support really unlimited?

Yes, on every package. Between sessions you can send me a paragraph you are stuck on, a new opening, a supplement you were not sure about, and I will answer. It is included in the price and it is genuinely one of the most used parts of working with me.

Is this allowed
Is it cheating to work with an essay coach?

No, and I want to be specific about where the line is. Your student is the author. I ask about their life until they say something that surprises them, then hand their own sentence back and we build from there. Sometimes that means working on a sentence together when they are stuck on one, the way a writing tutor would, but the memories, the voice and the thinking are all theirs.

Will the essay get flagged as AI?

This is the thing students worry about most now and it is a fair worry. Essays get flagged when they are vague, when every sentence runs the same length, and when the writer states a lesson instead of showing the moment it came from. My process pushes hard the other way, into specific detail and real remembered moments in their own vocabulary. I also run a pass specifically for this.

You are young. Why should we trust you with this?

Because I did this recently instead of decades ago. I know what the prompts look like right now, what the rules around AI are right now, and what a 17 year old will actually sit still for on a call. I have also coached more than 1,000 students since, which is more application essays than most people read in years.

We are not applying to Ivies. Is this still worth it?

Yes, and honestly it often matters more. Merit scholarships get decided on essays, and some of the largest awards my students earned came from schools that were never anyone's reach. A full ride to a school your child loves is worth more than a rejection from a famous one.

Who does not need this?

If your child's essay is already done, and it is specific, and three people you trust have read it and genuinely loved it, you do not need to book anything. I would rather tell you that than take the money.
