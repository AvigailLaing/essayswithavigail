The Method

The Yes Pile System

If you want to write an essay that goes in the yes pile, you have to write an essay with strategy, not just a story. This is the five stage system I run on every call, and it is the same one that got me into every school I applied to.

  1. 01
    Inventory
    What do I actually have to write about?

    We go through all seven Common App prompts out loud, no filtering and no judging, and I take notes live in a shared doc. You cannot pick a topic from a list of topics. You pick it from a list of moments.

    Worksheet: The Brain Dump
  2. 02
    Angle
    Which idea can carry 650 words, and what is it about?

    A story is not a thesis. This is where we find the one idea underneath your moment, the thing the essay is actually arguing about who you are.

    Worksheet: The Angle Finder
  3. 03
    Architecture
    What goes in each paragraph?

    Before you write a word, you know what every paragraph has to do and what it promises the next one. This is the step that stops the endless rewriting.

    Worksheet: The Outline Map
  4. 04
    Evidence
    Am I showing this, or just claiming it?

    We go line by line and replace anything you have asserted with something you can prove. This is also the pass that makes an essay read like a person instead of a machine.

    Worksheet: The Show-Don't-Tell Pass
  5. 05
    Screen
    Is this actually finished?

    The last read, against the same questions an admissions officer is asking. You submit knowing it is done, not hoping.

    Worksheet: The Final Screen

You leave with a finished essay and the five artifacts that produced it.

Every stage ships a worksheet you keep, so the system still works on the next essay, and the one after that.

Questions students ask
Is it cheating to work with an essay coach?

No, and I want to be specific about why. I never write a single word of your essay. That isn't a technicality, it's the whole method. I ask you about your life until you say something that surprises you, and then I hand your own sentence back to you and we build from there.

Admissions officers have said for years that outside feedback is fine, the same way a writing tutor or an SAT tutor is fine. What isn't fine is somebody writing it for you, and that's the one thing I won't do.

Will my essay get flagged as AI?

This is the thing students worry about most now, and it's a fair worry. Essays get flagged when they're vague, when every sentence runs the same length, and when the writer states a lesson instead of showing the moment it came from.

My process pushes hard in the opposite direction. We work in specific sensory detail, real remembered moments, and your actual vocabulary, because that's what makes writing read like a person. The words stay yours the whole time, so there's nothing there to flag.

I have no idea what to write about. Is that a problem?

That's the most common way students show up, and it's genuinely the best place to start. Most students have a story worth telling and just can't see it yet, because they're too close to it and because they assume it has to be impressive.

Finding it is the first thing we do together, and it usually takes one call.

What actually happens on a call?

There are two versions, depending on where you are.

If you don't have a topic yet, we get on a video call, I ask you about your life, and by the end you have a real idea and a full outline you can write from.

If you already have a draft, you send it to me beforehand and we go through it together on the call, line by line, and you leave with it actually revised instead of just critiqued. Either way you get a PDF afterward with everything we came up with.

Isn't all of this free on the internet?

A lot of it is, and I give plenty of it away myself. But no video can tell you which of your five stories is the one, and that's the actual job.

General advice can show you what a good essay looks like. It can't tell you what yours should be about.

My deadline is close. Is it too late?

The packages are built around 30 days because that's the pace that works best, but I've had students come to me a week out and still submit something they were proud of.

If your deadline is close, book a single strategy session and we'll make the most of the time you have.

I'm not in the US. Can we still work together?

Yes. I coach students across a lot of time zones, and every session is on video, so we just find a slot that works for you.

Questions parents ask
Why can't I just help my child with it myself?

You probably could, on the writing itself. The writing usually isn't the problem.

I've watched students who ignored a parent's feedback for three weeks make that exact change in ten minutes on a call with me, and it wasn't because the parent was wrong. It's that you're their parent. There's also the deadline, which matters more than people expect: a due date set by somebody outside the house tends to actually get met.

You're young. Why should we trust you with this?

Because I did this recently instead of decades ago. I know what the prompts look like right now, what the rules around AI are right now, and what a 17-year-old will actually sit still for on a call.

I've also coached more than 1,000 students since, which is more application essays than most people read in years. My own results and my students' results are both on this page, and they're the real answer to this question.

Who is this for, and who doesn't need it?

It's for students who either don't know what to write about, or have a draft that isn't landing and can't tell why.

If your child's essay is already done, and it's specific, and three people you trust have read it and genuinely loved it, you don't need to book anything. I'd rather tell you that than take the money.

Do you run the sessions yourself?

Every one of them. There's no team, no junior coach, and no handoff. When you book, you're booking me.

We're not applying to Ivy League schools. Is this still worth it?

Yes, and honestly it often matters more. Merit scholarships get decided on essays, and some of the largest awards my students earned this year came from schools that were never anyone's reach.

A full ride to a school your child loves is worth more than a rejection from a famous one.
